# Adopting SK1Kit

The look comes from how each screen is laid out, not from swapping controls one for one. An app whose native
controls have merely been restyled looks flat next to Skreener; one whose screens follow the recipes looks like it.
So work screen by screen, not control by control.

The full guide is the "Adopting SK1Kit in an app" section of SK1Kit's `DESIGN.md` (in the SK1Kit checkout, or
https://github.com/jsonify/SK1Kit/blob/main/DESIGN.md): the native → SK-1 table, the screen recipes, the rules and the
macOS pitfalls. Read it before writing code, and the component table above it for each part's API. `SK1Gallery`
(`Sources/SK1UI/Gallery/SK1Gallery.swift`) shows every part in use.

## Steps

1. **Add the package.** `.package(url: "https://github.com/jsonify/SK1Kit.git", from: "<latest tag>")` with the
   products `SK1Tokens` and `SK1UI` (XcodeGen: `packages:` plus target `dependencies:`). Use the newest tag: older
   ones lack parts (tiles, captioned keys, status strip, picker and knob arrived in 0.4.0). If the app commits an
   `.xcodeproj` generated by XcodeGen and XcodeGen isn't available, add the same entries to `project.pbxproj` by hand
   (`XCRemoteSwiftPackageReference`, two `XCSwiftPackageProductDependency`s, a Frameworks build phase with their
   `PBXBuildFile`s, `packageReferences` on the project, `packageProductDependencies` on the target).

2. **Inventory the screens.** List every window, popover, sheet, settings pane and overlay, and match each to a recipe
   in `DESIGN.md`: launcher (menu-bar popover), main window (captioned trays over an LCD), settings (groups of rows),
   notice card, sheet or popover. Decide for each: what goes on the LCD (live numbers, the current state), which
   commands form the trays and what they're captioned, and which single key is orange.

3. **Build each screen from its recipe.** Chassis on every root (each split-view column, sheet and popover), trays with
   captions, the LCD line, keys and tiles, `SKGroup`/`SKRow` panes for settings with knobs, pickers and switches. Put
   shared helpers (a countdown LCD, a settings pane) in one file in the app.

4. **Wire the hover status.** On a launcher or toolbar with an `SKStatusStrip`, give each `SKTile` and `SKCaptionedKey`
   an `onHover` that puts its name and shortcut on the strip, and clears it only if it is still the one shown.

5. **Audit.** Run `Scripts/sk1-audit.sh <app sources>` (from the SK1Kit checkout, or the copy
   `Scripts/install-adoption-kit.sh` put in the app). Fix each finding, or end the line with
   `// sk1-audit: ignore` when the native control is meant to stay (system menus and context menus, a full-screen
   overlay with its own look), and say why in the commit or PR.

6. **Check every screen against the rules** in `DESIGN.md`: chassis everywhere, one orange key, live numbers on the
   LCD, captioned trays, lamps for state, the audit clean. Look at each in light and dark.

## Things that went wrong before

- Restyling controls in place (a cream key where a button was) without changing the layout: the result didn't look
  like Skreener. Start from the recipe.
- The chassis on a `NavigationSplitView` didn't reach the sidebar, which draws its own material: put it on each
  column. A `Form` or `List` kept its own background until `.scrollContentBackground(.hidden)`.
- Two screens' main actions met on one screen and both were orange: demote the lesser to cream.
- A `Menu` with a custom label lost its drawing on macOS: use `SKPicker`.
- Without a Mac toolchain nothing was compiled. Say so plainly in the PR, and lean on SK1Kit's CI (it compiles the
  kit for macOS and iOS with warnings as errors and renders the gallery) for any new SK1Kit part.

## When a part is missing

If a screen needs an SK-1 part SK1Kit doesn't have (Skreener has it in `Retro*.swift`, `PillButton.swift`,
`MenuLauncher.swift`…), add it to SK1Kit rather than faking it in the app: port its drawing onto the internal
`SK1Draw` helpers, add it to the gallery and to `DESIGN.md`, and follow SK1Kit's `CLAUDE.md` (Conventional Commits,
warning-free, `#if canImport(SwiftUI)`).
